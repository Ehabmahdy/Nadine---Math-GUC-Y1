TOPIC 1

The real number line & inequalities

Big ideaNumbers grow to the right. An inequality isn't one number, it's a whole stretch of the line.
Open circle = endpoint not included. Filled dot = included.

Brackets: [ ] means the endpoint is included (≤, ≥). ( ) means it isn't (<, >). Infinity always gets ( ) because you never reach it.

The one extra rule: solving an inequality is like solving an equation, except when you multiply or divide by a negative number, you flip the sign. Press the button in the figure to see why: multiplying by −1 mirrors the line, so the shaded side swaps.

  • 1Solve −3x + 4 > 10
  • 2Subtract 4: −3x > 6
  • 3Divide by −3 and flip: x < −2 → (−∞, −2)
TOPIC 2

Absolute value

Big idea|something| means distance from zero, and a distance is never negative. |x − c| is the distance between x and c.

Every absolute value problem is one of three shapes. Picture walking a steps away from the centre:

|x| = a
exactly a steps away → two points
x = a or x = −a
|x| < a
closer than a → one piece in the middle
−a < x < a
|x| > a
further than a → two pieces outside
x < −a or x > a
Trap: |x| = −4 has no solution. A distance can't be negative.
TOPIC 3

Exponents & roots

Big ideaAn exponent counts repeated multiplication. Each step right multiplies by the base; each step left divides by it. That's why 2⁰ = 1 and 2⁻³ = 1/8.
Tap a bar to see its value
Bars use a ratio scale: every step is the same height because every step multiplies by the same base.
aᵐ · aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
multiply → add powers
x²·x³ = x⁵
aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ
divide → subtract powers
x⁷÷x² = x⁵
(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
power of a power → multiply
(x²)³ = x⁶
a⁰ = 1
zero power is always 1
5⁰ = 1
a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ
negative → flip to a fraction
2⁻³ = 1/8
a^(m/n) = (ⁿ√a)ᵐ
bottom = root, top = power
8^(2/3) = 2² = 4
Trap: √(a + b) is NOT √a + √b. Check: √(9+16) = √25 = 5, not 3 + 4 = 7.
TOPIC 4

Factoring polynomials

Big ideaFactoring is multiplying backwards. x² + 5x + 6 is the area of a rectangle; factoring finds its two side lengths, (x + 2) and (x + 3).
The two numbers p and q must multiply to c (the small corner square) and add to b (the two strips).

Always in this order:

  • 1Common factor first. 6x³ + 9x² = 3x²(2x + 3)
  • 2Count the terms and pick the pattern below.
  • 3Check by multiplying back out.
2 terms: a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b)
x² − 25 = (x − 5)(x + 5)
2 terms: a³ ± b³
x³ + 8 = (x + 2)(x² − 2x + 4)
3 terms: x² + bx + c
find p·q = c, p + q = b
4 terms: grouping
x³+3x²+2x+6 = (x + 3)(x² + 2)
TOPIC 5

Fractions & rationalization

Big ideaAlgebra fractions follow the same rules as number fractions. To add, cut both into equal-size pieces first (a common denominator).

Cancel factors, never terms. In (x² − 9)/(x + 3), factor first: (x − 3)(x + 3)/(x + 3) = x − 3. But in (x + 3)/3 the 3s can't cancel, because the top 3 is added, not multiplied.

Rationalizing removes a root from the bottom. Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate (same terms, opposite sign), since (a − b)(a + b) = a² − b² squares the root away.

  • 1Start: 4 / (√5 − 1)
  • 2Conjugate of √5 − 1 is √5 + 1
  • 3Bottom: (√5)² − 1² = 5 − 1 = 4
  • 4Answer: 4(√5 + 1)/4 = √5 + 1
